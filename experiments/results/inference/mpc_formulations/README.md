# MPC formulations for SIV

SIV has two distinct workloads. During private inference the parties must produce an activation that nobody holds in the clear. During a sampled audit, the datacenter already knows its model and trace, and the committee must decide whether they agree with an approved model and an authenticated record. Matrix multiplication admits a different shortcut in each workload.

| Workload | First choice to investigate | What remains expensive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Four-party private inference, one malicious party | Encode the input shares so that each party performs **one matrix product with one guard bit**. Keep the three-product signed-edge construction as a measured baseline. | Checked exchange of masked outputs, private nonlinearities, wider arithmetic |
| Sampled audit, up to three malicious committee members | Bind wide accumulator witnesses and use **two-sided random matrix checks** inside four-party SPDZ2k. Batch the output and nonlinear checks. They dominated the small complete-transition tests. | Ingesting the trace, public projections of secret shares, checks on nonlinear operators |
| Private inference, up to three malicious parties, no plaintext prover | Use a fixed private model and dealerless authenticated correlations to make online work public-times-secret. | Fresh model-dependent preprocessing for every query and layer |
| An architecture the parties can choose | Put learned private transformations in narrow subspaces, or compile a shallow polynomial network into coefficients that can be evaluated linearly on public features. | Expressiveness, checkpoint equivalence, and the privacy of the inputs and basis |

The strongest measured change to the existing runtime is the three-product formulation. It reduced full private prefill by 13–14% with four roles sharing one GPU. The one-product formulation has a working four-party CPU reference, but its extra communication rounds can outweigh its local arithmetic saving. For the audit, a random matrix check was 7.95× faster than scalar-triple recomputation at the largest matrix-only test. Including exact Q14 and ReLU at a smaller shape reduced the advantage to 2.7% after batching output checks on both paths. This points to jointly checking the affine and nonlinear relation as the next research question.

The [project design](https://docs.google.com/document/d/192TcAOZ43OQI1E3Q01Mxw7hRUGCXEL74rriaW2aI4ko/edit) calls for privacy with one honest computing party and correctness with abort. The [current Rep4 runtime](../../../../docs/protocol/protocol.md) hides the model and activations from one verifier. Two can reconstruct them. The [private audit](../../../../docs/protocol/private-rep4-audit.md) already has a prover who knows the model and trace, public inputs, committed boundaries and a private aggregate verdict. It currently recomputes integer affine/ReLU transitions and uses the Rep4 threshold.

## One matrix product per party with one guard bit

Consider an exact matrix product in the ring modulo $2^k$. Let the four additive components of the activation be X₀,…,X₃, and similarly for W. Party i holds every component except i. Each input component must already be consistent among its honest holders. Use a common unsigned lift of each component to the ring modulo $2^{k+1}$. In that wider ring, form two degree-one matrix polynomials

```text
F(t) = Σⱼ (t − j) Xⱼ
G(t) = Σⱼ (t − j) Wⱼ
H(t) = F(t) @ G(t).
```

Party i can form F(i) and G(i) locally because the coefficient on its missing component is zero. Its only secret-data matrix product is F(i) @ G(i). The coefficient of t² in H is the product of the two sums. Its low k bits are exactly X @ W, regardless of wrap in the individual lifts or accumulators. The parties can prepare and retain G(i) once per model. The inner dimension K appears in ordinary local matrix arithmetic. Network operations act on the output shape.

**Keep the product private.** For each output coordinate and each j, the three holders of component j generate fresh independent words Rⱼ and Sⱼ in the wider ring from their shared randomness. Define

```text
Pⱼ(t) = (t − j)(Rⱼ + t Sⱼ)
Y(t)  = H(t) + Σⱼ Pⱼ(t).
```

Each party i can produce Y(i) without the masks it is missing. Use a consistent authenticated broadcast for the four masked evaluations. All honest parties check the third difference before using any result. They also require an even second difference. Compute each indicated numerator modulo $2^{k+1}$ before dividing its canonical even representative by two.

```text
Y(3) − 3Y(2) + 3Y(1) − Y(0) = 0
C = (Y(2) − 2Y(1) + Y(0)) / 2  mod 2ᵏ
[X @ W] = C − Σⱼ [Sⱼ]          mod 2ᵏ.
```

Here each Sⱼ is already held by the right three parties. Negate those components and add the public C to one designated component. The surrounding program gets the same Rep4 output representation and can use its existing truncation and ReLU. A guard bit is necessary for this decoding rule. Modulo $2^k$, the polynomial $2^{k-1}t(t-1)$ is zero at every integer point and has a nonzero leading coefficient.

**Why the checks work.** The four coefficients in the third difference are odd. Any change to just one evaluation gives a nonzero third difference, including a change only to its top bit. Consistent broadcast matters because this argument assumes every honest verifier sees the same vector. For privacy against a fixed corrupt party i, its missing polynomial Pᵢ is uniform over *all* degree-at-most-two polynomials vanishing at i. Every such polynomial factors uniquely as (t−i)(R+tS), including over this ring. Once the corrupt party's own point is fixed, the other masked evaluations therefore disclose no further information about the product polynomial in this single-gate argument. Freshness across coordinates and gates and completing checks before dependent messages are part of the construction.

**Arithmetic and live protocol checks.** The CPU oracle exhaustively checked 4,672 small-ring quadratics and 260,864 unilateral evaluation changes. It also matched 414 full-width matrix output coordinates over nine widths including 16, 32 and 64 bits. A separate four-process CPU reference used mutually authenticated TLS, distributed seed setup and fresh masks. At k=16, 32 and 64, its shared output matched an integer oracle and fed SIV’s checked signed floor/ReLU. Corruption tests exercised consistent bad values, equivocation and malformed echo messages. For a 3×9 by 9×7 ring32 product, the full-echo broadcast used 3,804 global application bytes over two exchanges, excluding input and final output. A two-layer four-process test then evaluated private XW, exact Q14 floor/ReLU, a private down projection using Wᵀ, and final floor. It opened only the final result during the cascade. All 44 outputs matched at all four roles for each tested width. Its ring32 case used 29 exchanges with the existing exact activation code, excluding the final open. A proof for the composed protocol and a full GPU execution including its checked exchange remain open.

**Communication.** A second four-process CPU reference uses parties 0 and 3 as collectors. Each receives its own evaluation and information about both interior points. For any quadratic, Y(0) and Y(2) have the same low bit, as do Y(1) and Y(3). Each interior sender can therefore send a base-width low word to one collector and a base-width right shift to the other. The collectors check matching interior low words, recover the leading coefficient from their consecutive triples and check it agrees. The public correction is assigned to the component excluded from party 1. Only party 2 needs a data copy and an independent matching digest. A final all-role agreement gates use of the shares. The reference uses the four-party, one-fault case of [Lamport, Shostak and Pease’s oral-messages agreement](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2016/12/The-Byzantine-Generals-Problem.pdf).

For 21 ring32 outputs the optimized reference sent **576 application bytes in four exchanges**, consisting of 420 bytes of tensor data (five standard words/output), 96 digest bytes and 60 agreement bytes. Eight injected fault cases caused all honest parties to reject and one inconsistent status copy was corrected. Both CPU harnesses deliberately open the first raw product on the known fixture, then repeat with fresh shares for the floor/ReLU test. At full prefill output size the collector body would save 5 MiB across the two matrix gates versus Rep4’s six-word exchange, while adding round dependencies. This makes latency of the next dependent send part of the comparison. The full-echo version remains the simpler reference. Wider-ring interpolation has precedent in [MHz2k](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/1383), and subset-held random masks in [pseudorandom secret sharing](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/16467/). Historical novelty of this particular formulation is unestablished.

**Does one wider product actually buy time?** In paired RTX 4060 measurements, the three signed ring32 products described below took 2.275 and 2.782 ms for the expanding and contracting prefill matrices. One ring33 product using a level of Strassen took 2.123 and 2.390 ms. The trials include forming the query-dependent activation evaluation. They exclude masks, transport, validation and nonlinearities. A direct ring33 product without Strassen took 2.294 and 2.579 ms. Arithmetic on 33-bit random words occupies five byte limbs. The measured saving against signed pairs is **0.545 ms across both matrices**, despite the three-to-one product count. If four collector exchanges replace one exchange per gate on the critical path, six extra serial exchange latencies share that margin. Ignoring any bandwidth saving and all other overhead gives a break-even of about **91 μs per added exchange**. On a high-latency link, evaluation rounds can decide the winner.

## Three signed products using the existing Rep4 exchange

Index the global share components from 0 to 3. Give each unordered pair of component indices one contribution. For the matching pairs {0,2} and {1,3}, use −(Xᵢ−Xⱼ) @ (Wᵢ−Wⱼ). On the other four pairs use (Xᵢ+Xⱼ) @ (Wᵢ+Wⱼ). The six contributions sum to X @ W. Each ordered cross term occurs once. Each diagonal appears with coefficients +1, +1 and −1. The order of matrix operands never changes.

Each pair of components is jointly known to its two complementary parties. Both compute the same contribution and pass it to the existing checked joint-input protocol. A party holds three component pairs and computes three ordinary matrix products. In cyclic local slot order its weight operands are A=W₀+W₁, B=W₁+W₂ and B−A=W₂−W₀. Decode can store A and B alone. The exchange has the same participants, byte count and round schedule as the baseline. Its masked payloads encode different local values. This uses the shared-input primitive in [Fantastic Four](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec21fall-dalskov.pdf). The graph must retain the check-before-send barriers because delayed consistency checks have known security consequences. [Brüggemann and Schneider](https://encrypto.de/papers/BS26.pdf)

The reduction comes from changing what the holders jointly input. For the three original local coefficient matrices, an exhaustive test modulo two found that no span of four rank-one 3×3 binary matrices contains all three. Therefore four ring-linear GEMMs cannot preserve those exact three old outputs. Five suffice in SIV’s existing formulation. In the signed-pair scheme the three distinct pair contributions have independent cross terms, so three local GEMMs suffice and are necessary for that specific local map. The one-product gate changes the error check itself, allowing each party to compute a different local map.

## Check a committed batch of affine results inside SPDZ2k

For an audit, ask the datacenter to supply authenticated shares of a claimed **wide raw accumulator** Ẑ for each layer, before rescaling or ReLU. Bind every tensor to the agreed model, sampled record and predecessor/successor states before revealing any challenge. Work modulo $2^{64}$. The intended raw matrix result lives in the low 32 bits. The honest prover computes the full 64-bit product of the agreed lifts. Its high bits carry genuine ring32 wrap. For an affine bias, subtract the same bound bias from the claim. The downstream circuit must use the signed **low 32 bits** of that same authenticated witness.

Draw fresh public independent uniform row and column vectors u and r after commitment. The committee checks privately

```text
(uᵀ [X]) · ([W] r) = uᵀ [Ẑ] r  mod 2⁶⁴.
```

Both projections use public coefficients, so the parties perform them locally on their authenticated shares. A B×K by K×N claim leaves one private length-K dot product per probe. Stack committed prefixes for the same layer in the rows of X and Ẑ. They share a single projection of W per probe. This amortizes a weight scan across an audit batch. For several layers, draw independent vector blocks for each layer, sum their private residuals, and check one private zero per probe. This is the same test on a conceptual block-diagonal residual. Open only the aggregate verdict and any authorized output. For one row set u=1.

**Why five probes suffice for a false low word.** Write E=XW−Ẑ. If any low word is wrong, the smallest power of two dividing a nonzero entry has exponent v≤31. Put s=64−v≥33. After removing that power, some row has an odd entry, so its dot product with uniform r is uniform modulo $2^s$. If this row result has exactly h factors of two, random u annihilates the full vector with probability at most $2^{h-s}$. Averaging over h, including the case of a zero row result, bounds one probe by $(s+2)/2^{s+1}$. A single nonzero entry attains the bound. At s=33 five independent probes give less than $2^{-144.35}$ false acceptance for a fixed wrong low-word claim. The bound applies to the algebraic check. MAC security, proof binding, challenge generation and repeated attempts have their own bounds. A wrong high word alone can be harder to distinguish and has no effect on the low-word functionality. Fresh challenges still require an unbiasable, transcript-bound coin and a retry policy. Wider-ring one-sided compression is analyzed in [ZK-for-Z2K, Lemma 2.2](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1057.pdf).

For two prefill matrices with both operands private, five probes require **51,200 secret product terms**, against 4,294,967,296 terms in scalar-by-scalar recomputation. The parties still have 167,772,160 public-coefficient weight terms per value-share lane, plus authentication work, before batching across additional prefixes. Lifted raw witnesses occupy 10 MiB before distribution. A complete test must charge this traffic and the prover's work. When X is already public, direct X[W] needs no secret multiplications. Use hidden-to-hidden layers for the private-product comparison. Batching cannot detect a false transition that the audit never selects. If private inference has no participant who already holds the trace, obtaining these witnesses is a separate computation.

**Nonlinear and output checks.** For deterministic floor at scale S, check the signed relation $a=S q+e$ with a private range check $0≤e<S$. Bind q to the activation and enforce the signed ranges that rule out modular aliases. A second four-party SPDZ2k test checked the complete 2×3 by 3×2 deterministic Q14 affine/ReLU transition. It used exactly the same accumulator for the projections and the signed low-word bit decomposition. A genuine wrap passed. Changing the claimed activation, or changing the product and adapting the activation to it, failed. The four runs sent 380.698 MB each with fresh preprocessing, which illustrates the cost of the nonlinear checks on this tiny instance.

For stochastic Q14, bind the model and sampled input before drawing the agreed rounding coins, make those coins available to the witness producer under the intended disclosure rules, and bind its resulting trace before drawing independent projection challenges. Allowing the prover to pick either neighbor changes the distribution. The deterministic transition above has a different rounding contract. Range or lookup arguments can check many activation witnesses together. SIV already has a [private witness interface](../../../../docs/experiments/witness-activations.md), a [Q24 SiLU proof](../../../../docs/experiments/proof-activations.md), and an exact security boundary to replace when moving from Rep4 to SPDZ2k. [Range-Arithmetic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17623) develops a finite-field sum-check approach to rounded matrix multiplication and ReLU.

**The affine speedup shrinks once the decision includes ReLU.** On an 8×16 by 16×8 transition, direct recomputation derives its own accumulator and takes no wide witness or matrix challenges. The projected path ingests both. Both paths privately check the same claimed Q14/ReLU outputs and reveal one verdict. Medians below are for two honest four-party runs with fresh distributed preprocessing.

| Complete deterministic transition | Direct, time / aggregate MB | Projected, time / aggregate MB |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 64 separate output equalities | 6.372 s / 4,338 | 6.139 s / 4,245 |
| Four random checks of the 64 low-word outputs | 3.785 s / 2,600 | 3.681 s / 2,505 |

Batching output equality cut both elapsed times about 40%. It tests the intended 32-bit output semantics. Four independent uniform linear projections in the wider ring miss a fixed wrong low word with probability at most $2^{-132}$, in addition to the protocol’s own failure terms. Both paths rejected a flipped output sign bit. With these batched checks the remaining difference between direct and projected matrix arithmetic was only **2.7%**. The matrix-only comparison at the same shape gave about 2×. The size dependence and tiny trial counts matter. This comparison does not establish a speedup on a trained transformer.

**An exact affine–Q14–ReLU witness.** Instead of making the committee recover the bits of every accumulator, the prover can supply the nonnegative output h, negative quotient magnitude n, remainder r and a high-word carry Q. At S=16,384 require

```text
0 ≤ h < 2¹⁷,     0 ≤ n ≤ 2¹⁷,     0 ≤ r < S,     h·n = 0
wide affine witness = S(h−n) + r + 2³²Q    mod 2⁶⁴.
```

Use that expression as Ẑ in the matrix projection. Subtract a bound bias if the matrix check excludes bias. The ranges put S(h−n)+r in the signed ring32 interval. Complementarity makes h exactly the ReLU of its floor quotient. Since h·n is less than 2³⁴, a nonzero integer product cannot vanish modulo 2⁶⁴. The verifier can batch product checks, but must also authenticate and range-check every witness. For bounded probabilistic Q14 with uniform ρ in [0,S), use the same relation for the signed accumulator plus ρ and subtract ρ in the wide witness. Adding ρ must stay inside the signed range, as it does under SIV’s tighter probabilistic contract. A cleartext oracle covered every valid decomposition at small widths, 10,010 signed/wrapping fixtures at ring32, and 10,000 bounded stochastic fixtures. Efficient batched range checks and stronger backend security parameters are open experiments. This formulation gives the prover a concrete job that may remove expensive secret bit decomposition.

**Check the ML operation the verifier actually needs.** For a recorded log-probability L, observed token j and tolerance ε, let gᵢ=exp(zᵢ−m) using a stable shared shift and G=Σᵢgᵢ. The exact real-number test is

```text
exp(L−ε) · G  ≤  gⱼ  ≤  exp(L+ε) · G.
```

If L and ε are public, both thresholds are public. This replaces the private division and logarithm with two comparisons. The other logits can remain shared. The verifier still checks the exponentials, the vocabulary sum, and directed approximation errors near the acceptance boundary. A top-k shortlist needs a bound on the omitted mass. For example, with m equal to the maximum, a verified gap Δ for every omitted token bounds that mass by (vocabulary size − k) exp(−Δ). Merely revealing a shortlist could leak model outputs. Independently authenticated record selection remains necessary even when every arithmetic check passes.

The same reasoning applies to attention. For an agreed arithmetic specification with unnormalized weights Eᵢⱼ=exp(sᵢⱼ−mᵢ) on the allowed causal positions, the output O satisfies diag(E·1) O = E V. A prover can supply O. The committee can project this matrix identity and check the exponential witnesses without privately dividing every attention weight. A rounded intermediate probability in the approved model changes that identity and needs its own constraint. Materializing an entire sequence-by-sequence E can erase the gain. RMS normalization offers a smaller example: for positive integer v and agreed scale C, a claimed q=floor(C/√v) can be checked with q≥0 and q²v≤C²<(q+1)²v. These reformulations trade division and root finding for products, ranges and a clear error budget. They are proposed transformer tests beyond SIV's current FFN.

## Full private inference with a fixed model

For a resident private W, prepare fresh authenticated [R] and [RW] independently of the query. After X arrives, open D=X−R with authentication and compute [Y]=D[W]+[RW] locally. An additive SPDZ implementation needs a value GEMM and the corresponding public-times-MAC work. Every opening consumes its mask, including interrupted queries. Binding pools to the model, layer, dimensions and preprocessing epoch prevents accidental substitution or reuse. This works with all-but-one corruption if the underlying authentication and **distributed production of [R] and [RW]** achieve that threshold. The parties still collectively produce one masked matrix product per use.

Choose the protocol separately for expanding and contracting matrices. Opening the input mask is attractive at 2048→8192. Sending checked output contributions is attractive at 8192→2048. In a four-party CPU functional check using Rep4, the former exchanges 16 bytes per input coordinate plus 128 digest bytes. The current multiplication exchanges 24 bytes per output coordinate plus 192 digest bytes. On SIV's batch-128 expansion the calculated online payload falls from 24.0 to 4.0 MiB. The contraction reverses the advantage. The fresh packed pool for the expansion alone costs 15 MiB per party. These byte counts describe Rep4. An authenticated SPDZ opening uses its own representation and communication. A genuinely public first-layer input can multiply the secret weights locally already. [Rep4 protocol](../../../../docs/protocol/protocol.md) · [Fixed-secret-matrix amortization, Theorems 1–2, semi-honest setting](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/208.pdf)

A fresh four-party SPDZ2k run tested this identity directly for private X of shape 2×4 and private W of shape 4×3 with ring64 values. Eight authenticated mask elements and 24 fresh scalar triples generated [RW]. Preparing W, R and RW took 37.74 ms. Ingesting X, checking D and producing shared Y took 3.56 ms. All six checked outputs matched. Whole-process time including cold setup was 215.97 ms, with 7.56 MB global traffic. The run used a 64-bit SPDZ2k MAC parameter and a generic statistical parameter of 40. It measures a tiny live dealerless gate. Full-size replenishment remains unmeasured.

SIV previously implemented this fixed-model identity. The archived `86a4f5d:RESULTS.md` reports a four-party, warm-key Goldilocks LowGear run on a dependent 8→2→square→8 toy. Serving 2,400 one-use records cost 10.934 s for fresh pools, 16.056 s for the checked sequence, and 29.182 s process-inclusive. That was 12.159 ms/token or 1,705× the matched resident FP16 baseline. It excluded the cold HE key setup, and a whole-epoch statistical bound was unresolved. The old matrix-ring prototype at `86a4f5d:docs/matrix-spdz-reference.md` covered small square matrices with an experimental preprocessing provider. The [small-field MAMA notes](../../../../docs/protocol/native-mama-sacrifice.md) document a separate mask-privacy limitation. None supplies a production factory for this FFN.

## Model structure that makes MPC cheaper

**Hide parameters in a narrow learned subspace.** If the agreed model actually has W=W₀+UV with public W₀ and private factors of dimensions K×r and r×N, XW₀ is local on authenticated shares. Compute the private contraction XU with an output-sized protocol. Expand it through the fixed V with the fresh input-mask protocol above. Both exchanges now scale with **batch × rank**. With B=128 and r=16, the two existing Rep4 primitives would send about 80.3 KiB for this residual layer versus 4.0 MiB to mask the input to a full-width private W. This is an arithmetic estimate that excludes the nonlinearity, setup, checks outside the primitives and refill. Preserve the factorization and rounding boundaries in the agreed integer model. A truncated SVD of an arbitrary proprietary checkpoint changes what is being verified. A public base with private low-rank updates is an existing training convention. [LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)

**Compile the exact shallow square classifier.** SIV already has a trained model of the form score(x)=Σₕ sₕ(xWₕ+bₕ)²+c. It is exactly a quadratic xQxᵀ+xℓ+d with Q=W diag(s)Wᵀ, ℓ=2W diag(s)b and d=Σₕ sₕbₕ²+c. The model owner can provision authenticated coefficients tied to the approved checkpoint, or the committee can derive them offline. For public binary features, evaluating this private score only requires adding the shared coefficients selected by active feature pairs, followed by a private sign test. Fold the diagonal into the linear term. No intermediate feature or score needs to open. On the 512→4 checkpoint all 1,025 held-out scores matched exactly. The median example selected 66 linear/pair coefficients. The model-side price is 131,328 distinct nonconstant coefficients in the binary expansion, against 2,048 entries in the original encoder. Revealing feature locations would change the private-input classifier's contract.

For private x, a different implementation can open a fresh d′=x−r after preparing [f(r)] and [2rQ+ℓᵀ] for each query. Then evaluate f(x)=d′Qd′ᵀ+(2rQ+ℓᵀ)d′ᵀ+f(r) by public-times-secret arithmetic. The identity also matched all 1,025 scores modulo the classifier's 16-bit ring in a cleartext oracle. The masked d′ is dense, so this version pays quadratic local work per row. The four-hidden-unit factorization is a strong competitor. SIV's [classifier experiments](../text_classifier_report.md) show the value of moving rescaling. Absorbing scales into a polynomial model cut measured inference payload from 359.74 to 51.30 MB while changing the integer approximation. For an approved existing checkpoint, distinguish an exact rewrite from retraining or a tolerance change.

**Keep polynomial layers in small fields until the first operation that needs order.** Four byte-sized primes can represent the trained unscaled classifier's integer score by its residues. Every layer before the final sign is polynomial, so intermediate wrap in a residue lane is harmless. Only the final signed score must lie inside half the product of the primes. The small 512→4 model fits two primes. Checked CPU prototypes using private input and model sharing reproduced all 1,025 labels on each of the two checkpoints. A private carry test converted the final residue representation directly to a label. It matched the original label circuit's bytes and rounds on both models. At shape 8192×512×128 the current fused ring32 encoder, signed-pair ring32 and signed-pair four-prime kernels measured 1.298, 0.937 and 0.714 ms, respectively. These are isolated encoder times.

This choice also combines with the one-product construction. In an odd prime field, division by two is exact, so each party computes one **byte-input** GEMM per prime with no guard bit. A separate CPU composition reproduced the same 2,050 labels. Its simple echo-based exchange doubled the arithmetic message bytes relative to signed pairs, while the final private sign cost stayed unchanged. The cross-country choice depends on computation saved per transmitted output. In a deep Q14 ReLU network, sign and rescaling would repeatedly cross residue lanes, so the trained scale-free classifier is the stronger fit.

| Quad / PIGEON, one malicious party | Quad uses three online plus two preprocessing words per matrix output, and two persistent words per party. A local patched HPMPC CPU arithmetic harness found that selecting Q14 alone produced out-of-interval results. Applying SIV's signed range offset fixed standalone truncation. Adjusting the rounding of one offline summand also fixed the tested fused ReLU cases, with identical reported communication. These are functional checks of an experimental adaptation. Use full-width, fail-closed consistency checks and charge their rounds. | Three computing parties | On the trained ring64 Q12 16→64→8 fixture, six interleaved MP-SPDZ CPU runs per backend gave 39.7 ms and 4.64 MB for Rep4, 111.3 ms and 18.23 MB for three-party SPDZ-wise, and 156.2 ms and 28.38 MB for three-party post-sacrifice. | CRT or packed Shamir | Several small prime residues let byte-sized shares multiply directly. For 251, 241, 239 and 233 the product is 3,368,562,317. A no-wrap signed score up to 1,684,281,158 is recoverable. Exact sign and Q14 truncation need information from multiple residues. This is attractive for SIV's scale-free classifiers with only a final sign. A distinct ring-preserving option packs two weight columns in a quadratic Galois-ring extension, costing three half-width products and a checked degree reduction. The one-guard-bit scheme is the simpler candidate for a general Rep4 matrix gate. [MPC over rings](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/29216/) |

## Experiments that would change the choice

1. **For private inference at the current threshold**, compare the one-guard-bit gate against signed pairs on both SIV matrix shapes. Use canonical share lifts, fresh checked masks, and first implement consistent broadcast. Report packed and word-aligned bytes, rounds to the next dependent send, preprocessing, nonlinear time, and latency on separate links. Progress to the collector schedule only after verifying its transcript and abort behavior.
2. **For the all-but-one audit**, extend the tiny checked transition to one trained layer with independently approved model shares and an authenticated external trace. Charge model and trace binding, use the agreed rounding rule, then release one verdict. Batch prefixes fixed before the challenge. Compare against full SPDZ2k recomputation and a proof of the same committed relation. Report total traffic and both prover and verifier time.
3. **For all-but-one inference without a prover**, measure sustained production and consumption of fresh [R],[RW] on one expanding projection. Compare the actual serving rate and total work, including cold setup and exhaustion of a warm pool. This measurement decides whether model-dependent preprocessing fits SIV's audit cadence.

The [measurement archive](measurements.zip) contains the experimental harnesses, ring checks and run outputs. Its REPRODUCE.txt gives commands for the polynomial oracle, the four-process TLS tests, the classifier and the SPDZ2k transition. The stand-alone oracles use public fixtures. Timing evidence comes from local loopback or a single RTX 4060 Laptop GPU. None of the new constructions has a full audited implementation.
